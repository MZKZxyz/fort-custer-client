Try it with your hands.

Focus the maze and use arrows, hold a direction button, or drag anywhere on it. At a wall, keep contact and steer away. After clearing fire, lift before moving into the tile. Help always remains available.

State buttons replay ordinary directional commands through the existing input coordinator. They do not teleport the player or inject game state. Changing a design or state starts a fresh local demo.

What is—and is not—changing.

Baseline uses the untouched optimized /maze application. A, B and C are external DOM/CSS research overlays. Maze cells, artwork, circular cloud fog, reveal radius, game logic and server payloads remain the same.

No live account or backend is used. A deterministic 13 × 17 sample arrives through a local synthetic API. This is a design proposal, not a production implementation or deployment.

Why A leads.

It keeps the objective and single equipment slot visible, reserves a stable contextual line and puts detailed help behind a labelled control. At 320 × 568 the full maze and all direction buttons fit; B and C add some scrolling.

Classic large-grid desktop/landscape scrolling and tiny phone cells remain existing rendering/visibility limitations. This study does not quietly change that policy.
